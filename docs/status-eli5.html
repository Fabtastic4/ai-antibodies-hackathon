The game we are playing

Some sick cells have a little button on the outside, called HER2. There is already a medicine that works like a hand: it reaches out, grabs the button, and holds on, so the cell cannot carry on being naughty. That hand is called trastuzumab.

Our job today is a better hand. We may only change the fingertips — thirteen letters at the end of the loop that actually touches the button. And the new hand has to be properly new: if it looks like a hand someone has already tried, it does not count, no matter how well it works.

That last rule is the whole story of our day.

Grown-up version. Optimisation track, trastuzumab–HER2. The organisers' her2-novel-2026-11-09 weight redesigns the IMGT CDRH3 only; framework sequence adherence is 1.0, so every design shares trastuzumab's framework. Batch 1 is 100 designs, seed 42; the top 20 by generator score were refolded with Boltz-2 against the 1N8Z epitope.

Where all 400 hands ended up

Since this morning the team has made 400 hands across two batches. Here is what happens to them as each rule is applied.

We have 192 good, different hands and room for 20. Having enough allowed hands stopped being the problem some time this afternoon.

The problem is now the bottom bar. To put a hand on the list we have to say how well it grips and whether it survives being warm — and right now not one of the 192 has both numbers. The program that writes the final list checks, finds them missing, and refuses to write anything. It is right to refuse: a list ranked on the one number we do have would be a guess wearing a scoreboard's clothes.

Grown-up version. 141 of 200 eligible in b1-novel-s1, 70 of 200 in b2-explor-s2-fixed; 211 eligible, 192 unique CDRH3 loops, no overlap between batches. Neither batch's gate run actually reached abguard — both *-gate.stdout files say abguard not found — so these are the offline check's numbers, cross-validated against the committed eligible lists (batch 1: their 140 is a strict subset of our 141; batch 2: both 70, differing by one each way) and against abguard team-validate on the refolded 20. score/rank.py exits 3 because min_ipae and predicted_ddg are absent for every candidate — two of the three judged axes. At the measured 15–17 s/design, a shortlist refold is roughly 20 minutes of compute.

Why the best hands died

You might think the thrown-out hands were bad copies. The opposite is true.

Long ago, other scientists built half a million hands and wrote down, for each one, whether it held on well, so-so, or badly. For five of our six rejected hands, the nearest already-measured hand was one of the good ones.

So our model is not making junk. It keeps re-inventing hands that work — which is exactly why the referee throws them out.

We can now check this ourselves, before spending anything

The referee lives on the big computer and we only get answers from it in batches. So this afternoon we rebuilt its novelty test to run on a laptop. On these 20 hands it gives the same verdict on every single one — the same 14 yes, the same 6 no — in 38 seconds, with nothing to install.

That changes the order of our work: we can now throw away the doomed hands before paying for the expensive grip test, instead of after.

Grown-up version. scripts/novelty_distance.py, standard library only, 20 designs × 525,027 references in 38 s. 20/20 agreement with abguard team-validate. It gets there by re-extracting every reference CDRH3 from its vh_sequence (the segment between the conserved Cys and the WGxG motif) rather than reading the cdrh3 column, which holds the 10-mer core — the exact trap docs/abguard.md §2 warns about, and the reason a naive check would have passed designs abguard rejects. Output: reports/b1_novelty_distance.csv.

Where the good hands live

Now the awkward part. This picture takes all 524,313 measured hands, sorts them by how far they are from the original medicine, and shows what fraction of each group held on well, so-so or badly.

held on well so-so held on badly numbers on top = how many hands in that group

Stay close to the original and almost everything works: 9 out of 10 hands that are three fingertips away held on well. Wander far away and it falls apart — out at nine or ten fingertips, most hands are duds.

So the two rules pull in opposite directions. Grip wants us to stay near the original. Novelty forbids us from being near anything already tried — and the things already tried are mostly packed around the original. We have to thread between them.

Grown-up version. FACS binding class by CDRH3 core edit distance from wild type. At d3: 1,341 high / 105 medium / 44 low = 90.0% high, n = 1,490. At d9–d10: low dominates. This is the quantitative version of the trade-off the scatter above shows qualitatively, and it is the argument for seeding from measured HIGH binders at exactly the gate distance rather than sampling the loop freely — the 1,341 HIGH variants at d3 are the pond to fish in.

How good can a hand even get?

For 182 of those old hands, someone measured exactly how tightly it holds on — a real number from a real machine, not a computer guess. Each little blue line is one measured hand. Further left is a tighter grip.

Two things to see. Most hands people tried are far worse than the medicine we already have — the middle of the pack is about three thousand times weaker. Changing the fingertips usually makes things worse.

But look at the very best one. Someone already found a hand that beats the real medicine, and only just: 0.0234 against 0.0285. It is three fingertips away from the original. So a better hand definitely exists — and we now know roughly where to look for it.

Grown-up version. SPR KD, 182 unique CDRH3s with quantified binding (190 rows). Median 86 nM against trastuzumab's own 0.0285 nM; max 13,863 nM. Best variant WGSQGFYAYD at 0.0234 nM, 3 core substitutions from wild type (G3S, D4Q, M9Y) — a measured, better-than-WT anchor at exactly the gate distance. Any design seeded 3 edits off it is eligible by construction against that sequence, and has to be re-checked against the rest of the database.

What the model actually changed

Each of the thirteen fingertips is a letter. The top row is the original medicine's letters. The bar shows how many of our 20 hands kept that letter, and underneath, where the model rewrote it, the letter it liked instead.

never touched mostly kept mostly rewritten

The model is tidy about it. It leaves the two letters at the start and the two at the end completely alone — those hold the shape of the loop. It rips out the middle, positions four to eight, in nearly every design. Position six loses its original letter in all twenty.

One change is worth pointing at. Position eleven in the real medicine is M, and M is a letter that goes off — it reacts with oxygen and the medicine spoils. In 16 of our 20 hands, the model put a F there instead. We did not ask it to. It is on the referee's own list of things that make a medicine fragile.

Grown-up version. Per-position retention of SRWGGDGFYAMDY across the 20 refolded designs. Positions 1, 2, 12, 13 are invariant (the SR…DY frame); the weight varies 9 of the 10 core positions. Position 11 M→F in 16/20 removes the MetOx motif from abguard's LAP liability reference (medium severity, and one of only three motifs that discriminate between our designs — the rest are inherited framework and identical across all 20). Worth stating in the pitch as an observed property of the output, not as an intended optimisation: nothing in our generation spec asked for it.

Three rules we had written down wrong

This morning we found the referee's actual instruction book on the big computer and read the code instead of the slides. We had copied three rules down wrong — and all three had been making our job harder than it really was.

1. We were changing one fingertip too many

We thought the rule was "at least four changes". It is three. Every extra change costs grip, so we were giving grip away for nothing.

2. We were counting the wrong fingertips

We had the fingertip list as 11 letters, and the measured tables use a 10-letter version. The referee uses 13. Counting with a short list makes a hand look newer than it is — and then the referee says no.

3. The size limit is per hand, not both together

A two-part design gets 250 letters each, not 250 shared. A whole constraint we had been designing around does not exist.

Grown-up version. There is no 70% CDRH3-identity test; whole-Fv identity only selects which bar applies (≥ 0.70 → 3 mutations, < 0.70 → 2). The IMGT CDRH3 is the 13-mer SRWGGDGFYAMDY, not the structure's 11-mer or the FACS tables' 10-mer core; mistake 2 is now defended against in code, not just in prose. MAX_SEQUENCE_LENGTH = 250 is a per-column check. All three read from the installed wheel — docs/abguard.md.

What is still missing

Saying the gaps out loud is worth more points than pretending there are none. In order of what actually blocks the hand-in.

  • Nothing can be handed in until the grip numbers exist. This is the only true blocker. The ranking program needs two numbers per hand and has neither, for any of the 192. Measured speed is about 15 seconds a hand, so a shortlist is roughly twenty minutes of computer time. Until that runs, the hand-in sheet cannot be written at all.
  • Does the hand survive being warm? One of the three judged properties, and no program on the platform measures it. We have stand-ins only, and the talk has to say so out loud rather than hope nobody asks.
  • The brand-new-hand route is still empty. All 400 hands rewrite the old hand's fingertips. The other route needs the big computer and has nothing committed. If we are not entering it, the talk should say that we chose not to, not leave it looking forgotten.
  • Nobody has asked how to rank. The referee's sheet has no rank column. We think it is row order. Two minutes with an organiser settles it.
  • A novelty check we can run ourselves. Done. It agrees with the referee on all 20 refolded hands, and on both batches' own lists across 400 more. This was the biggest hole this morning.
  • Both batches checked against the measured pile. Neither batch had actually been — both gate runs quietly failed to find the referee and fell back to comparing against the original medicine alone. Now done properly: 211 of 400 survive.
  • One repository again. The team's work had split into two lines that never met. Merged, with every conflict resolved by keeping both sides' findings.
