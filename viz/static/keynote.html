The challenge

Three measurements decide the score

Every tested design is compared with trastuzumab itself. Beat it on all three for S tier, and teams are ranked on S-tier count first.

Axis 1

Binding to HER2

Measured by SPR.

Axis 2

Epitope preservation

Competitive binding against trastuzumab.

Axis 3

Thermostability

Measured directly.

So the goal was never volume. It was designs that plausibly move all three at once.

Adaptyv measures three things for every tested design: binding by SPR, whether it still hits the same epitope, and thermostability. Three out of three is S tier, and teams rank on S-tier count first. Ten designs that each improve one thing lose to two that improve all three.

Novelty and affinity pull in opposite directions. The gate pushed us further from trastuzumab than affinity alone would choose.

The rules say three CDRH3 mutations from trastuzumab. But abguard counts against every one of 610 thousand known CDRH3s. At three or four mutations, zero of twelve passed. Five, 44 percent. Six or more, 79 to 89 percent. That reshaped the campaign.

Measured epitope contact spread the field from 0.58 down to 0.00. The eight are demoted to ranks 13–20 and labelled, not hidden.

Our first draft ranked on a paratope proxy, and it gave 18 designs the same top score. Then the co-folds came back. Eight of 18 put the antibody somewhere else on HER2 entirely. Six of those eight were designs the proxy rated best. So we re-ranked on what the co-fold measured.

Boltz-2 reads 0.13 higher on average, and the rank orders are close to unrelated.

An ipTM ranking inherits whichever model produced it. So we ranked on where the antibody lands, and kept ipTM as a cross-check.

We scored all 210 eligible designs with OpenFold3 and 139 with Boltz-2 as well. Correlation 0.15. If you rank on ipTM you are really choosing a model. We ranked on whether the co-fold puts the antibody on the epitope.
